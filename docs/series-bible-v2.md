# RAINY DAY — Series Bible (v2)

> **v2 (Episode 2 onward):** the series now leans hard into **survival and social collapse**. The quake was only the
> start; the real story is what happens when help doesn't come. Episode 1 ("DAY 0: THE SHAKING") stands exactly as made.
> v1 (`docs/series-bible-v1.md`) is kept unchanged for reference.

An episodic stick-figure survival series about the days after a magnitude-9 Cascadia earthquake and tsunami cut a small
Washington coastal town off from the world, told one day at a time: **DAY 0, DAY 1, DAY 2…**

## Logline

When the Cascadia Subduction Zone finally breaks, Drizzle — a cheerful, over-prepared neighbor who never takes off his
yellow rain slicker — and Mags, the gruff retiree next door with a hand-crank radio, survive the shaking and the waves.
Then the harder part starts: no roads, no power, no phones, no rescue — and a hilltop full of frightened neighbors who
have to decide what they owe each other.

## Format

- **Episode:** ~5–6 minutes (YouTube, 16:9, 1920×1080), released alongside **4 vertical parts** (~75 s each, never under
  65 s) for TikTok/Shorts (1080×1920). Every part ends on a hook or cliffhanger.
- **Structure:** each episode covers one in-story day ("DAY 0: THE SHAKING"). Title card at the open, "NEXT: DAY N" sting
  at the close.
- **Audio:** episodes are first made captions-only (v1). Every composition carries a reserved, clearly marked narration/VO slot and a
  music/SFX slot so voice and sound can be added later without re-cutting (Episode 1 has voiced versions).
- **Captions:** three kinds — *narration* (soft gray, lower-key), *dialogue* (speaker name in slicker yellow), and
  *signs/lessons* (League Gothic, yellow) for on-screen survival takeaways and in-world text.

- **Recap:** from Episode 2 on, Part 1 opens with a 3–5 s recap of the previous day.

## Characters

### Drizzle (main character)
- **Look:** an ink stick figure in a bright **yellow rain slicker with the hood up**. The slicker is the show's visual
  signature and the only saturated color on screen. **It never comes off** — indoors, in bed, under a table. Toggle
  buttons down the front; often wears his slate-gray go-bag.
- **Personality:** warm, upbeat, a little goofy, easily distracted, braver than he thinks. Copes with fear through
  jokes ("You never know when it'll rain in here"). Learns fast, and by the end of each episode is the one reminding
  others.
- **Arc:** from "prepared on paper" (a go-bag he calls his *snack bag*) to genuinely ready — and then to the harder
  lesson of v2: being prepared makes you visible. His go-bag makes him the most-watched person on the hill, and he has
  to work out what he owes the people who brought nothing.
- **Running gags:** the slicker; snacks as a survival priority; treating the furniture as a friend after it saves him.

### Mags (sidekick)
- **Look:** an older ink stick figure with a gray bun, round glasses, a muted gray-blue cardigan with a pocket, slight
  stoop. Always carries a **hand-crank emergency radio**.
- **Personality:** practical, gruff, dry, unsentimental, secretly soft on Drizzle. Former harbor-master's office clerk;
  remembers the 1964 tsunami stories and every drill since. Gives orders in three words or fewer when it matters.
- **Function:** the voice of correct practice — she corrects myths (doorways, driving, waiting for alerts) in character,
  never as a lecture. In v2 she is also the camp's conscience and its best source of news: her hand-crank radio is the
  only window to the outside, and she decides what to say about what she hears.

### Gord Pike (recurring, introduced in Episode 2) — the self-appointed leader
- **Look:** a big, broad muted-gray stick figure in a flat newsboy cap and a puffy steel-gray vest, with a stubble
  beard. **Signature item: a clipboard** (with a clipped pen) that never leaves his hand.
- **Who:** harbor-district council member and owner of a charter fishing boat (now somewhere on Main Street). Loud,
  organized, genuinely wants to keep people alive — and genuinely wants to be the one deciding how.
- **Function:** order versus control. Proposes pooling everything into a common pile that he rations; leads the
  supply run into the flood zone against Mags' warning. Not a villain: right about some things, dangerously wrong
  about others, and he will not back down in front of a crowd.

### Tess Varga (recurring, introduced in Episode 2) — the quiet stockpiler
- **Look:** a slim stick figure in a knit beanie with a pom, a ponytail, and a long dark coat. **Signature item: a big
  duffel bag with a padlock on the zipper,** which she sits on, sleeps beside, and never puts down.
- **Who:** single mom of Milo (8). Grabbed a packed bag when the shaking started and won't say what's in it.
- **Function:** sharing versus protecting your own. Refuses Gord's pile; the camp's suspicion lands on her first. Her
  reasons (her son's medication is in that bag) are only revealed later. Drizzle sees himself in her.

### Milo Varga — Tess's son, 8. Quiet, watches everything, attaches himself to Drizzle (and his snacks).

### Recurring townspeople (light touch, drawn as muted gray stick figures)
Ms. Ortega (school teacher), Dale (gas-station owner — missing since Day 0), the Pruitt twins (ferry deckhands, one of
them joins Gord's supply run), Captain Lund (ferry), assorted neighbors.

## Setting: Gull Harbor, Washington (fictional)

A small town (pop. 2,140) on the outer Washington coast.
- **The low harbor:** the town's heart and its danger — main street, storefronts (diner, hardware store, post office),
  the **ferry dock** with the little island ferry, all a few meters above sea level, inside the tsunami hazard zone.
- **The hill to the east:** the only high ground within walking distance. A marked **evacuation trail** switchbacks up
  through Douglas firs to the **hilltop assembly area** (canopy, water tank, picnic table).
- **The school:** on the lower slope of the hill; its kids drill the evacuation every year.
- **The gas station:** **Dale's Gas & Grocery**, at the east end of Main Street — the first traffic jam on Day 0, and on
  Day 1 the nearest stock of bottled water and food, sitting in the flood zone.
- **The hilltop camp (from Day 1):** the assembly area becomes a camp — tents, tarps strung between cars, a cracked
  water tank, a missing-persons board, a cold fire ring, and the picnic table that becomes Gord's "office".
- **Weather:** almost always raining. Stormy blues and grays everywhere; the only warm color is Drizzle's slicker.

## Premise and world rules

A full-margin **magnitude 9** rupture of the Cascadia Subduction Zone. The show follows real public guidance
(Washington EMD, USGS, FEMA, NOAA) and keeps the science honest:
- Strong shaking lasts **minutes** (roughly 3–5), not seconds.
- On the coast the **shaking is the warning**; the first tsunami wave can arrive in **15–30 minutes**, before any
  official alert. Power is out; sirens may not sound.
- Tsunamis come as a **series of waves**; the first is often not the largest; danger lasts many hours.
- Roads and bridges fail; Gull Harbor is cut off for days. The town must look after itself.

**v2 world rules — no one is coming (yet):**
- **No outside help.** Highway 101 and the Gull Creek bridge are down, the coast road is cut in both directions, the
  harbor is wreckage. The whole coast from northern California to British Columbia was hit, so help is spread thin
  and will come by air and sea first, days to weeks out.
- **No power, no cell service, no internet.** The only news is fragments on Mags' hand-crank radio — an **information
  vacuum** that rumor fills.
- **Dwindling resources.** Most people fled with nothing. Water runs out first; food, warmth, and medicine follow.
- **Trust erodes.** Neighbors who helped each other on Day 0 start counting what others have. Small groups form, make
  their own rules, and decide who's "in".
- **Moral choices have no clean answers:** share or protect your own; follow the loud leader or the right one; go
  back down for supplies or stay safe and thirsty. The show doesn't preach — it shows consequences.

## Tone

**Tense, grounded, and serious,** with Drizzle's humor as the pressure release (and Mags' deadpan as the release
valve's lid). Peril and conflict are shown through **tension, standoffs, and consequences** — raised voices, a hand on a
bag, a crowd going quiet, smoke rising from the town, an empty spot where something used to be — and through the
**environment** (water where the street used to be, a broken bridge, a cracked tank). **No gore, no on-screen killing,
no graphic injury, no on-screen deaths.** People can be missing, hurt off-screen, or in danger; we cut away, and we
always show the cost.

## Teaching rules

- Every episode teaches **2–3 real survival lessons**, woven into the story through what characters *do*, with a short
  on-screen *lesson caption* at the moment it matters — never a lecture scene.
- Show the wrong instinct, then the right action (Drizzle heads for the doorway; Mags sends him under the table).
- Recap the episode's lessons once near the end, in character.
- Lessons now include the **social** side of survival (rationing, keeping supplies discreet, accountability, de-escalation)
  alongside the physical (water, shelter, carbon monoxide, floodwater). Keep facts accurate to public guidance (CDC,
  FEMA/Ready.gov, EPA, Washington EMD); flag anything uncertain.

## Visual style (see `assets/style/rainy-day-style-v1.css` and `assets/characters/rainy-day-rig-v3.js`)

- Hand-drawn ink line art on stormy blue-gray grounds; pen-wobble outlines doubled with a faint second pass; hatching for
  shadow; 8 fps "line boil" on characters.
- Palette: night `#141B26`, storm `#24303F`, slate `#3A4A5E`, steel `#5B6E85`, mist `#8FA0B4`, fog `#C3CDD8`,
  paper `#E6EAEE`, ink `#10161F`; **slicker yellow `#F2C230` is the only saturated color.**
- Line weights: figures 8 px, object outlines 4 px, detail 2 px (at 1920×1080).
- Type: Kalam (captions, dialogue), League Gothic (day cards, signs, lesson captions).

- Episode 2+ light: a **flat, cold gray day** (paler sky than Episode 1's storm, low fog, constant drizzle). Night
  scenes go darker, lit only by fire and flashlights.
- New characters stay in the muted grays; each has **one signature item** so they read at TikTok size (Gord's
  clipboard, Tess's padlocked duffel). Yellow still belongs to Drizzle alone.

## Season 1 arc — 8 episodes (v2)

1. **DAY 0: THE SHAKING.** *(made — unchanged.)* An ordinary rainy morning in Drizzle's kitchen turns into a
   five-minute earthquake. Drop, cover, hold on; go-bag, not belongings; walk uphill the moment the shaking stops. From
   the trail they watch the sea drain and the waves take the lower town. On the hilltop the radio reports the Highway
   101 bridge is down — no road access. A light on the water.
2. **DAY 1: NO ONE IS COMING.** A cold gray morning on the hill; the town below is wrecked and flooded, and no rescue
   is anywhere in sight. Mags' radio picks up fragments: the whole coast was hit; help could be days or weeks away.
   Panic. Gord Pike takes charge with a clipboard and counts supplies — most people brought nothing, and Drizzle's
   go-bag makes him the center of attention. Gord demands a common pile; Tess refuses to open her padlocked duffel.
   Against Mags' warning, Gord leads a group down to Dale's Gas & Grocery for water. Lessons: water first (assume tap
   and flood water are unsafe; boil or purify; the water-heater tank), stay out of the flood zone, never run stoves or
   generators in enclosed spaces (carbon monoxide), ration food from day one and keep supplies discreet. Cliffhanger:
   an explosion and black smoke at the gas station — and back at camp, Drizzle's go-bag is gone.
3. **DAY 1 (NIGHT): THE FIRE BELOW.** Who came back up the hill, and who didn't. Two of Gord's group return with burns
   and a story that doesn't add up; Dale's store is burning and one Pruitt twin is missing. The camp needs someone to
   blame for the go-bag, and Tess is the easy answer. Drizzle and Mags run a headcount and treat burns while Gord
   hardens. Lessons: burns first aid (cool running water, cover loosely, no ice or butter), the buddy system and
   accountability, staying warm through a wet night (hypothermia). Cliffhanger: Milo shows Drizzle where his go-bag
   really is — inside Gord's tent.
4. **DAY 2: THE RULES.** Gord announces camp rules: a guarded supply tent, curfew, and "contributors eat first." Drizzle
   has to decide whether to confront him in front of everyone. Tess finally opens the duffel for Drizzle alone: Milo's
   insulin. Drizzle, Mags, Tess and a few others quietly form their own small group. Lessons: sanitation (latrines away
   from water and camp), medication storage without power, de-escalation and how to say no to a crowd. Cliffhanger: the
   supply tent is raided overnight, and Gord blames "the ones who wouldn't share."
5. **DAY 3: ACROSS THE CREEK.** Signal fires on the next hill: another group of survivors from the school side, cut off
   by the flooded creek. Rumors fill the vacuum — looters, a rescue ship, a second quake. Gord wants the trail blocked.
   Drizzle and Mags make contact with signals and the radio. Lessons: visual signals (SOS, mirrors, three fires),
   never crossing moving floodwater ("turn around, don't drown"), checking rumors against the radio. Cliffhanger: a
   small boat lands on the beach below with strangers who want to trade.
6. **DAY 4: STANDOFF.** The strangers come up the hill; Gord's group meets them with cars across the trail. A tense
   standoff over water, food and fuel. Drizzle talks both sides down to a trade — and learns what everyone is really
   short of. Lessons: barter and de-escalation, CO and fuel safety around generators, safe food without refrigeration.
   Cliffhanger: Mags collapses at the radio — her heart medication went down with her house.
7. **DAY 5: THE PRICE.** The strangers' boat has a medic and a pharmacy kit; Gord forbids any more "giving away camp
   supplies." Drizzle has to choose between the rules, his own recovered go-bag, and Mags. Tess backs him; the camp
   splits in two. Lessons: keep a 7-day+ supply of medication and a written list, recognizing heart-attack warning
   signs, community skills inventories. Cliffhanger: Gord and his group take the supply tent and the trail and declare
   the hilltop "closed."
8. **DAY 6: THE CRANK.** With the camp divided, Drizzle cranks Mags' radio all night and reaches a fishing fleet
   relaying for the Coast Guard. Relief arrives by sea and air — and both halves of the camp have to agree who goes
   first (the sick, the kids, the old). Gord gives up the clipboard. Lessons: how relief actually arrives when roads are
   cut, two-week readiness, building community before the disaster. **Season cliffhanger:** as the first boat unloads,
   the radio reports the aftershock sequence is migrating north, and the buoy at the harbor mouth just registered a rise.
   Drizzle pulls his hood tighter. *"…Here we go."*
