An episodic stick-figure survival series about the month after a magnitude-9 Cascadia earthquake and tsunami cut a
small Washington coastal town off from the world.

## 1. Logline and season shape

When the Cascadia Subduction Zone breaks, Drizzle — a cheerful, over-prepared neighbor who never takes off his yellow
rain slicker — and Mags, the blunt retiree next door with a hand-crank radio, survive the shaking and the waves. Then
the long part starts: no road, no power, no phones, no rescue — thirty days on a hilltop with two hundred neighbors,
watching cooperation turn into rationing, rationing into factions, and factions into a test of who they really are.

The season moves through five phases, the way real disasters often do:

| Phase | Episodes | What the camp feels like |
|---|---|---|
| The event | 1 (DAY 0) | Instinct, fear, the right actions in the first minutes |
| The first day and night | 2–3 (DAY 1, DAY 1 · NIGHT) | Shock; nobody coming; first rules; first blame |
| Cooperation ("disaster community") | 4 (DAY 4) | People organize, help, share — hope |
| Scarcity | 5 (DAY 9) | Shortages, rumors, the first person who leaves |
| Factions → breakdown | 6–7 (DAY 16, DAY 23) | Control vs. shared decisions; theft; outsiders; rule of the strongest |
| First contact | 8 (DAY 30) | Help arrives by sea/air; who goes first; Season 2 hook |

Episodes 1–3 are told hour by hour. **From Episode 4 the day counter jumps** (see §3).

## 2. Season 1 — episode by episode

Format per episode: ~5 min 16:9 + four 9:16 parts (≥65 s each), each part ends on a hook; title sting → DAY card →
(from Ep 4) the "since last time" montage + tracker cards; NEXT sting at the close. 2–4 lessons per episode, shown
through action with a short lesson caption, recapped once in character near the end.

### Ep 1 — DAY 0: THE SHAKING · *The quake* — **published, do not change**
Morning coffee at Drizzle's; Mags drops by for sugar and to check his go-bag (his "snack bag"). The five-minute quake:
Drizzle heads for the doorway, Mags sends him under the table. Go-bag, not belongings. The harbor drains; no siren; they
walk (not drive) uphill; an aftershock on the trail; Mags' knees slow her and Drizzle gives her his shoulder; the radio
warning arrives twenty minutes late; the waves take the lower town, Mags' house with it. On the hilltop: "three things…
and four: snacks." The radio: the Highway 101 bridge is down. *Cliffhanger:* a light on the water.
Lessons (published): drop, cover, hold on; on the coast the shaking is the warning — walk uphill now; go-bag, not stuff.

### Ep 2 — DAY 1: NO ONE IS COMING · *No one is coming* — **published, do not change**
Cold gray morning; the town below is gone and nobody is coming (radio: the whole coast was hit; days, maybe weeks).
Panic; a neighbor tries to drive out; Mags stops a man drinking runoff; Drizzle's rain catch and boiling; Mags stops a
stove going into a tent. Gord Pike takes charge with a clipboard and counts supplies — Drizzle's open go-bag makes him
the center of attention; Gord wants "one pile, I ration it"; Tess won't open her padlocked duffel. Against Mags'
warning Gord leads four down to Dale's Gas & Grocery for water. *Cliffhanger:* black smoke over Dale's — and back at
camp, Drizzle's go-bag is gone.
Lessons (published): water first (boil/purify; the water-heater tank); stay out of the flood zone; never run a stove or
generator in an enclosed space (CO); ration food from day one, keep supplies discreet.

### Ep 3 — DAY 1 · NIGHT: THE FIRE BELOW · *First night* (last episode told hour by hour)
**Plot.** At dusk three of the four come back up the hill: Gord with one case of water, and Roy and Dana with burns.
**What they found:** Dale's store half-flooded and stripped, the water pallet under a collapsed shelf, fuel vapor over
the lot — and when the downed line sparked, the fuel went up. Cal Pruitt went back in for a second case and didn't come
out the front; nobody saw where he went. Gord's version ("Cal ran out the back, he'll turn up") and Dana's ("you told
him to grab the water first") don't match. Cal's twin, Wes, wants to go down in the dark; Mags: "First light. With a
buddy." With no first-aid kit (it was in the stolen bag) Drizzle and Mags treat the burns with the water-heater water;
Tess quietly hands over gauze — and the camp decides she must be the thief. Drizzle stands between Tess and the crowd.
The rain turns cold and Roy, soaked, starts to shiver badly: dry clothes, off the ground, a hat, a warm sweet drink,
no booze. At the lip, Wes flashes three blinks down at the dark town; far below a light blinks back — twice. Late at
night Milo leads Drizzle to Gord's tent: the go-bag is inside, on top of the water everyone else is rationing.
**Who took it and why:** Lyle Kemper, Gord's most loyal follower, who stayed up the hill — on Gord's standing
instruction that "anything that big goes to the supply tent." Gord's reason, which he believes: *"One bag like that
and people fight over it. I was keeping the peace."* Drizzle's answer is the episode's last line before the hook:
*"The first-aid kit was in it. Roy needed it."*
**Lessons:** burns — cool running water ≥10 min, no ice/butter, rings off, cover loosely, don't pop blisters;
headcount + written list + buddy system after any incident; hypothermia on a wet night — get dry, insulate from the
ground, cover the head, share body heat, warm sweet drinks if alert, never alcohol; don't accuse without evidence.
**Cliffhanger:** Gord's flashlight snaps on behind Drizzle and Milo: *"Can't sleep, Drizzle?"*
**Drives it:** Gord, Wes, Dana, Roy, Tess, Milo, Lyle (unseen until the reveal).

### Ep 4 — DAY 4: THE CAMP · *Cooperation*
**Plot.** First light on Day 2 went well, and the montage shows it: Wes, Drizzle and two buddies roped together bring
Cal down off the hardware-store roof with a broken ankle — the two blinks were him. By Day 4 the hilltop is a working
camp. Ms. Ortega's older students run messages and fill water jugs; Benny (the dad from the stove scene) runs a
boiling station; Dana runs a first-aid corner — stocked, by Drizzle's own choice, with his go-bag's first-aid kit
("It's not a snack bag anymore. It's the camp's."). Mags and Drizzle lay out a latrine downhill and well away from the
water. Gord, embarrassed after Day 1, is genuinely useful: he organizes shifts and a sign-up sheet. Tess, still
guarded, shows Drizzle alone what's in the duffel: Milo's insulin. Narration names what's happening: in most disasters,
people help each other first. *Cliffhanger:* the radio again — a fragment about "relief prioritized for population
centers" — and Gord quietly takes the list of who has what off the board and into his tent.
**Lessons:** sanitation — latrine at least ~200 ft / 60 m from water and camp, downhill, wash hands with soap or
sanitizer after every use; boiling (rolling boil 1 min) and household-bleach disinfection for clear water (see §9);
early disaster communities usually cooperate — organize shifts and give everyone a job; mark and protect the water
source.
**Drives it:** Ms. Ortega, Benny, Dana, Wes and Cal, Tess and Milo, Gord (at his best).

### Ep 5 — DAY 9: THE LONG WAIT · *Shortages*
**Plot.** Montage: the rain stops for days (a cold dry high), the rain catch is empty, the water heaters are drained,
the bleach is almost gone, the food table is half bare. Ration arguments start; Gord's "workers get full rations" rule
splits families. Darla Finch spreads a radio rumor — "the Army's evacuating everyone at the school hill" — and twenty
people almost leave for a place that doesn't exist; Mags makes everyone wait for the next real broadcast. Mags insists
on carrying water up from the creek herself and her knee gives out on the trail; she is laid up, furious and in pain,
and for the first time Drizzle has to run the water station without her (comic at first — then he gets it right).
Hank Dorsey, who wanted to drive out on Day 1, packs to walk inland alone. Drizzle can't stop him; he makes him leave
his route on the board and take a whistle. *Cliffhanger:* Hank's whistle, three blasts, far off in the trees at dusk —
then nothing.
**Lessons:** don't ration *water* below what you need — ration food (people can go far longer without food than
without water); food that touched floodwater goes in the trash (only undamaged commercial cans can be cleaned and
sanitized); check rumors against an official broadcast before acting; if anyone leaves, leave a route, a time and a
signal — three of anything means distress.
**Drives it:** Mags (her knee — the mid-season vulnerability), Drizzle stepping up, Darla, Hank, Gord.

### Ep 6 — DAY 16: TWO CAMPS · *Factions*
**Plot.** Montage: a tent town now — cleared paths, a garden plot of cut-and-replant potatoes and cabbage starts
salvaged from yards, laundry lines, clothes visibly worn. Two sides: Gord's "supply committee" (Lyle, Darla, the
workers who eat first) and the people who want decisions made together at the canopy (Ortega, Dana, Wes, Benny, Tess,
with Drizzle as the reluctant face and Mags, back on her feet with a cane, as the voice). The first serious theft: the
camp's last bleach and a box of food bars disappear in the night; each side blames the other. Then a small fishing
boat runs up onto the beach below: RENNER and a handful of people from a cut-off village up the coast, with fish, a
water filter, and a working marine radio. They want fuel and medicine. Renner trades fairly — and asks too many
questions about who's in charge. *Cliffhanger:* Drizzle sees Renner talking quietly into her radio — and the stolen
food bars' wrappers are on the floor of Lyle's lean-to.
**Lessons:** keep a written inventory and a two-person rule for shared supplies; cuts and wounds after floodwater —
clean, cover, and watch for infection signs (redness spreading, swelling, warmth, pus, red streaks, fever) — tetanus
risk; trade with strangers carefully and in the open; generators and stoves stay outdoors and well away from openings.
**Drives it:** Gord vs. the canopy group; Lyle (the thief); Renner (arrives); Tess; Mags with the cane.

### Ep 7 — DAY 23: THE STRONGEST · *Breakdown*
**Plot.** Montage: fewer supplies on the table, thinner faces, a fence of tarps and cars around Gord's side. Gord
announces the rules: the supply tent is guarded, Lyle has the only key, "contributors eat first," no one uses the boat
without his say. Milo gets sick: his insulin, kept as cool as Tess can manage, is running out and the last vial is
past the time it stays good at room temperature. The only way to get more is Renner's boat, two hours up the coast to a
clinic her people say still has stock — and the boat needs the camp's last fuel, which Gord controls. Nobody wants to
spend the fuel on one kid. Ms. Ortega has been secretly giving her rations to her students and faints at the water
line. Drizzle — who by now keeps the whole canopy side going with jokes, shift songs and the nightly "good news /
bad news" — has to choose: steal the fuel, or stand up in front of everyone and ask. He asks. The vote is close, and
it goes his way only when Benny and Darla cross over. *Cliffhanger:* the boat leaves at dawn with Tess, Milo, Renner
and Wes — and Gord and Lyle are standing on the beach with the fuel cans they kept back.
**Lessons:** insulin and medicine without power — keep below room temperature, out of sun and never frozen, know how
long your supply stays good once out of the fridge; warning signs of high blood sugar (extreme thirst, frequent
urination, nausea, confusion) mean urgent care; oral rehydration solution for diarrhea; look after each other's minds
too — routine, sleep, checking in (psychological first aid).
**Drives it:** Drizzle (his humor now holds people together), Tess and Milo, Gord and Lyle (hostile), Ms. Ortega,
Benny and Darla (the swing votes), Renner.

### Ep 8 — DAY 30: FIRST CONTACT · *First contact* (season finale)
**Plot.** Day 30 opens on the empty sea. The boat isn't back. Mags cranks the radio every hour; Drizzle has the kids
build a giant ground signal on the bare hilltop out of tarps and rocks. On the third night a Coast Guard helicopter
sees the signal fire; at dawn it lands on the cleared zone — and with the crew is Hank, who walked inland, made it to
a relief point, and told them two hundred people were on the hill. Then the boat comes around the headland with Tess,
Milo (on fresh insulin) and a relief vessel behind it. The crew can take only the most vulnerable first; the camp has
to agree who: Cal, Milo and Tess, Ms. Ortega, the oldest — Mags refuses her seat. Gord hands over his list to the crew
and insists to the end he was right: "Somebody had to." Drizzle stays; he and Mags watch the first flight leave.
*Season 2 hook:* Drizzle finds Renner's radio log. She's been reporting the camp's numbers to someone every night since
she arrived — and it wasn't the Coast Guard. Below, her boat is already gone. *"…Here we go."*
**Lessons:** signaling for rescue — three of anything (fires, whistle blasts, flashes) and big ground-to-air signals (a
"V" means "need assistance", an "X" means "need medical help"; SOS works too); preparing a helicopter landing zone (flat,
clear of wires and loose objects, approach only when the crew signals); triage for evacuation — the most vulnerable go
first; two-weeks-ready households and knowing your neighbors before the disaster.
**Drives it:** Drizzle, Mags, Hank (returns), Tess and Milo, Gord, Renner (the mystery).

## 3. Time-jump devices (from Episode 4)

### 3.1 The DAY card + "since last time" montage
- Every episode opens (after the cold open and title sting) on a **big DAY card**: League Gothic, slicker yellow, the
  same treatment as Episode 2's "DAY 1" card — `DAY 4`, `DAY 9`, `DAY 16`, `DAY 23`, `DAY 30` — with a small subtitle.
- Then a **5–8 second montage** of what changed since the last episode: 4–6 hard cuts of ~1.2 s each, no dialogue, one
  narration line over it ("Three days. No helicopter. But the camp had a toilet."). Each cut shows one concrete change
  (a new structure, an empty shelf, a new face, a missing one).

### 3.2 Recurring resource tracker cards
Three stacked cards, paper on a dark panel, League Gothic numbers, shown right after the montage and updated once more
when a number changes on screen. **WATER LEFT** and **FOOD LEFT** count days of *stored, safe* supply at the camp's
current use; **PEOPLE IN CAMP** is the hilltop headcount (Ms. Ortega's roster).

| Episode | DAY | WATER LEFT (days) | FOOD LEFT (days) | PEOPLE IN CAMP | Why it moved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 4 | 6 | 10 | 212 | pooled supplies + rain catch + water-heater tanks; Cal brought up (+1) |
| 5 | 9 | 2 | 6 | 205 | dry spell empties the rain catch; bleach nearly gone; Hank and 6 others walk inland (−7) |
| 6 | 16 | 3 | 4 | 226 | rain returns + Renner's water filter (water +1); theft; 21 outsiders arrive by boat |
| 7 | 23 | 1 | 2 | 219 | filter clogs, fuel for boiling nearly gone; a family follows the outsiders back up the coast (−7) |
| 8 | 30 | ½ → relief | 0 → relief | 219 → 196 | fishing and foraging only; first evacuation flight takes the most vulnerable (−23) |

The numbers always come down between episodes except where the story earns a rise (rain, the filter). WATER is the
one that matters most and it is never rationed below need on screen — the drama is in making more of it.

## 4. Character roster

The rule stands: supporting characters are drawn in the muted grays with **one signature item each**; slicker yellow
belongs to Drizzle alone. Scale = helpful / indifferent / hostile **at the start of the season** (their first
appearance) → where they end by Episode 8.

### 4.1 Core

**DRIZZLE** — main character, comic relief. Yellow rain slicker with the hood up, never off (indoors, in bed, under a
table). Optimistic, clumsy, over-prepared in silly ways (snacks as a food group, raincoat indoors, a "snack bag" that
turns out to be a real go-bag). He copes with fear through jokes. **Arc:** Eps 1–3 his jokes are how he avoids being
scared; Ep 4 he gives his go-bag to the camp; Ep 5 he has to run the water station when Mags is down; by **Ep 7 his
humor is how he keeps people going** — the nightly "good news / bad news", shift songs, the kid who laughs at the
latrine sign — and his biggest moment is a serious one (asking the camp for the fuel). His jokes never undercut a
survival lesson: the joke lands *before* or *after* the lesson line, never on it.
*Voice:* "Good news: I'm still waterproof. Bad news: so is the latrine. Shift starts at six."

**MAGS** — the voice of wisdom. Older, practical, dry, sees trouble before anyone else. Gray bun, round glasses,
cardigan, hand-crank radio. Teaches in short, blunt lines ("The shaking WAS the alert."). Her house went down with the
lower town on Day 0. **Mid-season vulnerability — her knees** (seeded in Episode 1: "Give me a second. These knees are
old."): in Ep 5 one gives out on the creek trail and she is laid up; for the first time she can't be the one who goes,
and Drizzle has to step up. She returns in Ep 6 with a cane and has to teach through him. She refuses an evacuation
seat in Ep 8.
*Voice:* "Water first. Then opinions."

### 4.3 Supporting cast in detail

**1. Gord Pike — the organizer who becomes the controller.** Loud, organized, genuinely wants people to live — and
genuinely wants to be the one deciding how. On Day 1 he's the first to stand on the table and calm people; by Day 1's
evening he's taken the water and Drizzle's bag "for safekeeping." Ep 4 shows him at his best (shifts, a sign-up
sheet), which is why his slide hurts. Ep 6 he runs the "supply committee"; Ep 7 he makes the rules by strength.
**End:** does not change his mind; hands the list to the rescue crew and says "Somebody had to." *Never a villain on
paper: right about some things, dangerously wrong about others, and he won't back down in front of a crowd.*
*Voice:* "One pile. I ration it. Fair for everyone." (Ep 2) · "Nobody touches the fuel without my say."

**2. Tess Varga and Milo — guarded, then the camp's honest ledger.** Tess grabbed a packed bag when the shaking
started and won't say what's in it; the camp suspects her first. Ep 4 she shows Drizzle alone: Milo's insulin. Ep 7 is
her worst day — Milo's supply is running out and she has to ask the camp for help it doesn't want to give. **Lands:**
helpful — in Ep 8 she is the one the canopy side trusts with the shared supply ledger, and she hands the padlock key to
Dana. Milo attaches himself to Drizzle (and his snacks); he is the one who saw the bag go into Gord's tent.
*Voice — Tess:* "Mine. For my son." (Ep 2) · *Milo:* "I saw where your snack bag went."

**3. Ms. Ortega — the teacher.** Practiced the evacuation every year; got every kid up the hill on Day 0. Calm,
organized, uses her teacher voice on adults too. Runs the headcount roster (PEOPLE IN CAMP). Ep 7 she faints at the
water line — she has been giving her rations to her students. Stays helpful; first evacuation flight in Ep 8 (she
argues; Mags wins).
*Voice:* "Line up by twos. Buddy's name, out loud. Adults too."

**4. Wes and Cal Pruitt — the twins.** Ferry deckhands, identical caps. Cal went on Gord's run (Ep 2: "Gord… you smell
that?") and is missing after the fire; Wes refuses to wait (Ep 3) and leads the first-light rescue that finds Cal on
the hardware-store roof (Ep 4). Wes becomes Drizzle's most loyal helper and goes on the insulin run in Ep 7. Cal, with
his ankle splinted, is one of the first flown out.
*Voice — Wes:* "Three blinks. Cal and I did it as kids."

**5. Dana Reyes — the volunteer who burns out.** A home-health aide who went on the Dale's run, came back with a burned
forearm, and the next week was running first aid with Drizzle's donated kit. Tired, kind, short with people who waste
her time. By Ep 7 she is exhausted and snapping — the cost of being the helpful one — and Mags makes her sleep.
*Voice:* "You told him to grab the water first." (Ep 3) · "Sit. Hand out. Don't look."

**6. Lyle Kemper — the follower who becomes the enforcer.** On Day 1 he is sure help will come ("They'll come. They
have to."), drinks runoff ("It's just rainwater."), and is the first to back Gord against Drizzle ("Yeah! Why should he
keep it?"). He carries the bag to Gord's tent in the first hour of the fire, steals the bleach and food bars in Ep 6,
holds the supply-tent key in Ep 7. **Never changes.** Threat is only ever tension: the flashlight held like a baton,
standing in a doorway, a hand on a strap.
*Voice:* "Gord says it's locked. So it's locked."

**7. Hank Dorsey — looks out for himself.** Waited for a phone alert on Day 0 ("Huh. No alert on my phone yet…"),
tried to drive out on Day 1 ("I'm driving out. I'm not waiting."), walks inland alone on Day 9. Not cruel — he just
doesn't believe in groups. His leaving is what saves them: he reaches a relief point and comes back with the crew on
Day 30. Still jangling the keys to a car that's buried in mud.
*Voice:* "No offense. I'm not dying on a hill for a clipboard."

**8. Benny Ochoa — his kid first.** The dad who carried a lit stove toward the tent on Day 1 ("My boy's freezing. Just
for a few minutes."). Learns from it; by Day 4 runs the boiling station, stove outside and proud of it. Votes with
whoever feeds his son — until Ep 7, when he crosses to Drizzle's side for Milo.
*Voice:* "Rolling boil, one minute. I've got a timer and nothing else to do."

**9. Darla Finch — fear, out loud.** The woman who said "We'll be dead in weeks!" on Day 1. Checks a dead phone out of
habit; repeats whatever she last heard. Starts the Ep 5 evacuation rumor; sides with Gord in Ep 6 because he sounds
sure; in Ep 7 she sees Ms. Ortega collapse and crosses over. Indifferent → hostile → back.
*Voice:* "Somebody heard on the radio — the Army's at the school. Everyone's going."

**10. Renner — the wildcard.** Arrives on Day 16 with a small fishing boat, a handful of people from a village up the
coast, fish, a water filter and a working marine radio. Weathered, calm, trades fairly, laughs at Drizzle's jokes, and
asks too many questions about who's in charge and what the camp has. Takes Tess and Milo up the coast for insulin in
Ep 7 (and brings them back); in the finale her radio log shows she's been reporting the camp to someone who isn't the
Coast Guard. Her boat was the light on the water at the end of Episode 1. Never resolved in Season 1.
*Voice:* "Everybody's from somewhere, Drizzle. Who decides things up here?"

**Required spread check:** helpful — Ms. Ortega, Dana, Wes (and Milo, Cal); indifferent/self-focused — Hank, Benny,

## 5. Dialogue style rules

1. **Lines are short and readable as captions:** one idea per line, ideally ≤ 8 words, never more than ~12; a caption
   holds 2–4 s. If a line needs a comma and a second clause, split it into two lines or two speakers.
2. **Every voice is specific.** Drizzle: hopeful questions and self-deprecating jokes ("I officially hate the ground.").
   Mags: imperatives in three words or fewer when it matters ("Up. Keep moving."). Gord: "Folks!", lists, ownership
   words (mine, my say, my list). Tess: as few words as possible. Lyle: borrows Gord's authority ("Gord says…"). Hank:
   "no offense". Darla: repeats what she heard. Renner: questions back.
3. **Drizzle gets the laughs; Mags gets the lessons; supporting characters carry the conflict.** A lesson line is
   never a joke and never undercut by one; the joke comes right before or right after.
4. **Show the wrong instinct, then the right action** (the doorway, the runoff, the stove in the tent).
5. **No gore, no on-screen killing, no graphic injury, no on-screen deaths.** Threat comes through tension, standoffs
   and consequences: raised voices, a hand on a bag, a beam in a face, a crowd going quiet, an empty spot, a name under
   MISSING. Burns and injuries are implied (a hand held up, a wrap, a splint). People can be missing or hurt off-screen;
   we always show the cost.
6. **Radio lines are fragments** with ellipses ("...relief may take days... possibly weeks..."); they are the only news.
7. **Lesson captions** are imperative, ALL CAPS, ≤ 12 words, and accurate to public guidance (§9).

## 9. Survival facts used in v3 (sources: CDC, FEMA/Ready.gov, EPA, FDA, American Red Cross, WHO, WA EMD, ICAO)

Accurate as stated (general public guidance):
- **Burns:** cool (not ice-cold) running water for at least 10 minutes; no ice, butter or ointments on a fresh burn;
  remove rings/tight items before swelling; cover loosely with a clean non-stick dressing or cling wrap; don't break
  blisters; large, deep, or face/hand/genital burns need medical care. (Red Cross; some guidance says 10–20 min.)
- **Hypothermia:** move out of wind and wet, replace wet clothing with dry, insulate from the ground, cover the head,
  warm the core, share body heat; warm sweet drinks only if fully alert; no alcohol. (CDC)
- **Boiling:** rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft). (CDC)
- **Bleach disinfection of clear water:** unscented household bleach, about 8 drops per gallon for 6% bleach
  (6 drops for 8.25%), stir, wait 30 minutes; double for cloudy water after filtering. (EPA) — *on-screen numbers must
  name the bleach strength or say "follow the label".*
- **Latrines:** dig/place them at least ~200 ft (60 m) from water sources and away from camp; handwashing after use.
  (CDC/backcountry guidance)
- **Water vs. food rationing:** don't ration drinking water below need — drink what you need today and look for more
  tomorrow; ration food. (Ready.gov) Plan 1 gallon per person per day.
- **Floodwater and food:** throw out food that touched floodwater; only undamaged commercial cans/retort pouches can be
  kept after removing labels, washing and sanitizing. (FDA/USDA)
- **Wounds after floods:** clean with safe water and soap, cover, watch for infection signs; tetanus risk — get a
  tetanus shot if it's been more than 5 years for a dirty wound. (CDC)
- **CO:** never run generators, grills or stoves indoors or in tents; generators at least 20 ft from windows/doors.
  (CDC)
- **Signals:** three of anything (fires, whistle blasts, flashes) = distress; ground-to-air "V" = require assistance,
  "X" = require medical assistance. (ICAO ground-air visual signal code)
- **High blood sugar warning signs:** extreme thirst, frequent urination, nausea/vomiting, fruity breath, confusion →
  urgent medical care. (CDC/ADA)
- **Oral rehydration (home recipe):** 6 level teaspoons sugar + ½ teaspoon salt in 1 liter of safe water. (WHO)
- **Disaster communities:** research on disasters consistently finds that cooperation and helping are the norm in the
  first days; panic and looting are much rarer than people expect. (disaster sociology — e.g. Quarantelli; Solnit)

Flagged — keep vague on screen or check before use:
- **Insulin at room temperature:** most insulin vials/pens are good for about **28 days** once out of the fridge
  (59–86 °F) — but it **varies by product** (some 14–56 days). On screen say "it only lasts a few weeks out of the
  fridge" rather than an exact number.
- **"Three days without water"** is a rule of thumb, not a fact; don't put a number on screen.
- **Helicopter landing zone size** (often given as ~100 × 100 ft for a small helicopter) varies by aircraft; on screen
  say "flat, clear, no wires, no loose tarps."
- **30 days without outside help** in Gull Harbor is a story choice; WA EMD asks for "2 Weeks Ready" and warns some
  coastal areas could wait longer. Episode 8 narration should say so.
- **Garden plot by Day 16:** nothing planted on Day 4 is edible by Day 16 except sprouts/greens; the garden is about
  morale and replanting salvaged starts — don't show a harvest.
- **Renner's clinic two hours up the coast with insulin stock** — fiction; keep it a "her people say".
