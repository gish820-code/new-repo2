# RAINY DAY — Series Bible (v1)

An episodic stick-figure survival series about the day a magnitude-9 Cascadia earthquake hits a small Washington
coastal town, told one day at a time: **DAY 0, DAY 1, DAY 2…**

## Logline

When the Cascadia Subduction Zone finally breaks, Drizzle — a cheerful, over-prepared neighbor who never takes off his
yellow rain slicker — and Mags, the gruff retiree next door with a hand-crank radio, have to get themselves and the town
of Gull Harbor through the shaking, the waves, and the long days after.

## Format

- **Episode:** ~5–6 minutes (YouTube, 16:9, 1920×1080), released alongside **4 vertical parts** (~75 s each, never under
  65 s) for TikTok/Shorts (1080×1920). Every part ends on a hook or cliffhanger.
- **Structure:** each episode covers one in-story day ("DAY 0: THE SHAKING"). Title card at the open, "NEXT: DAY N" sting
  at the close.
- **Audio (v1):** captions only. Every composition carries a reserved, clearly marked narration/VO slot and a
  music/SFX slot so voice and sound can be added later without re-cutting.
- **Captions:** three kinds — *narration* (soft gray, lower-key), *dialogue* (speaker name in slicker yellow), and
  *signs/lessons* (League Gothic, yellow) for on-screen survival takeaways and in-world text.

## Characters

### Drizzle (main character)
- **Look:** an ink stick figure in a bright **yellow rain slicker with the hood up**. The slicker is the show's visual
  signature and the only saturated color on screen. **It never comes off** — indoors, in bed, under a table. Toggle
  buttons down the front; often wears his slate-gray go-bag.
- **Personality:** warm, upbeat, a little goofy, easily distracted, braver than he thinks. Copes with fear through
  jokes ("You never know when it'll rain in here"). Learns fast, and by the end of each episode is the one reminding
  others.
- **Arc:** from "prepared on paper" (a go-bag he calls his *snack bag*) to genuinely ready — and to looking after people.
- **Running gags:** the slicker; snacks as a survival priority; treating the furniture as a friend after it saves him.

### Mags (sidekick)
- **Look:** an older ink stick figure with a gray bun, round glasses, a muted gray-blue cardigan with a pocket, slight
  stoop. Always carries a **hand-crank emergency radio**.
- **Personality:** practical, gruff, dry, unsentimental, secretly soft on Drizzle. Former harbor-master's office clerk;
  remembers the 1964 tsunami stories and every drill since. Gives orders in three words or fewer when it matters.
- **Function:** the voice of correct practice — she corrects myths (doorways, driving, waiting for alerts) in character,
  never as a lecture.

### Recurring townspeople (light touch, drawn as muted gray stick figures)
Ms. Ortega (school teacher), Dale (gas-station owner), the Pruitt twins (ferry deckhands), Captain Lund (ferry), assorted
neighbors.

## Setting: Gull Harbor, Washington (fictional)

A small town (pop. 2,140) on the outer Washington coast.
- **The low harbor:** the town's heart and its danger — main street, storefronts (diner, hardware store, post office),
  the **ferry dock** with the little island ferry, all a few meters above sea level, inside the tsunami hazard zone.
- **The hill to the east:** the only high ground within walking distance. A marked **evacuation trail** switchbacks up
  through Douglas firs to the **hilltop assembly area** (canopy, water tank, picnic table).
- **The school:** on the lower slope of the hill; its kids drill the evacuation every year.
- **The gas station:** Dale's, at the east end of main street — the first traffic jam when people try to drive out.
- **Weather:** almost always raining. Stormy blues and grays everywhere; the only warm color is Drizzle's slicker.

## Premise and world rules

A full-margin **magnitude 9** rupture of the Cascadia Subduction Zone. The show follows real public guidance
(Washington EMD, USGS, FEMA, NOAA) and keeps the science honest:
- Strong shaking lasts **minutes** (roughly 3–5), not seconds.
- On the coast the **shaking is the warning**; the first tsunami wave can arrive in **15–30 minutes**, before any
  official alert. Power is out; sirens may not sound.
- Tsunamis come as a **series of waves**; the first is often not the largest; danger lasts many hours.
- Roads and bridges fail; Gull Harbor is cut off for days. The town must look after itself.

## Tone

Tense but warm. Peril is shown through the **environment** — rattling dishes, a swaying lamp, a harbor draining to mud,
a gray line on the horizon, water where the street used to be. **No gore, no graphic injury, no on-screen deaths.**
People are scared, then brave, then funny again. Humor comes mostly from Drizzle and from Mags' deadpan replies.

## Teaching rules

- Every episode teaches **2–3 real survival lessons**, woven into the story through what characters *do*, with a short
  on-screen *lesson caption* at the moment it matters — never a lecture scene.
- Show the wrong instinct, then the right action (Drizzle heads for the doorway; Mags sends him under the table).
- Recap the episode's lessons once near the end, in character.

## Visual style (see `assets/style/rainy-day-style-v1.css` and `assets/characters/rainy-day-rig-v1.js`)

- Hand-drawn ink line art on stormy blue-gray grounds; pen-wobble outlines doubled with a faint second pass; hatching for
  shadow; 8 fps "line boil" on characters.
- Palette: night `#141B26`, storm `#24303F`, slate `#3A4A5E`, steel `#5B6E85`, mist `#8FA0B4`, fog `#C3CDD8`,
  paper `#E6EAEE`, ink `#10161F`; **slicker yellow `#F2C230` is the only saturated color.**
- Line weights: figures 8 px, object outlines 4 px, detail 2 px (at 1920×1080).
- Type: Kalam (captions, dialogue), League Gothic (day cards, signs, lesson captions).

## Season 1 arc — 8 episodes

1. **DAY 0: THE SHAKING.** An ordinary rainy morning in Drizzle's kitchen turns into a five-minute earthquake. Mags gets
   him under the table (drop, cover, hold on), stops him packing his life into his arms (go-bag, not belongings), and —
   because the power is out and no siren sounds — leads the walk uphill the moment the shaking stops. From the trail
   they watch the sea drain from the harbor and the first wave take the ferry dock. On the hilltop the radio reports the
   highway bridge is down: Gull Harbor is cut off. Out on the dark water, a red flare.
2. **DAY 0 (NIGHT): THE SECOND WAVE.** The hilltop crowd wants to go back down for pets, cars, and medicine; Mags and
   Ms. Ortega hold the line as larger waves arrive after dark. Drizzle organizes headcounts and a missing-persons board,
   learns why you stay up high for hours, and signals the flare with a flashlight — someone answers from the capsized
   ferry. Lessons: tsunamis are a series of waves; stay on high ground until officials say it's safe; check in and
   account for people. Cliffhanger: the answering light goes out.
3. **DAY 1: WATER.** Dawn reveals the flattened lowland. With the water tank cracked by an aftershock, Drizzle and Mags
   ration what's left, collect rain off tarps, and boil it, while the Pruitt twins paddle out to the ferry and find
   Captain Lund alive. Lessons: store water (a gallon per person per day), treat questionable water, rainwater
   catchment. Cliffhanger: Lund says there were nine passengers; they've found six.
4. **DAY 2: THE GAS STATION.** A search for supplies in the tsunami zone during a lull — Dale's gas station smells of
   gas. Drizzle learns to shut off a leaking supply only if trained, to avoid sparks and flames, and to stay out of
   damaged buildings. Lessons: gas-leak safety, never enter damaged structures, buddy system. Cliffhanger: an aftershock
   traps Drizzle in the station's walk-in cooler, and his radio battery is dead — but Mags' radio is hand-crank.
5. **DAY 3: SIGNALS.** Phones are useless; Mags runs a ham-radio relay and the town builds a giant SOS on the hilltop.
   Drizzle learns text-before-call, the out-of-area contact plan, and signal mirrors. Lessons: family communication
   plans, out-of-area contacts, visual signals for aircraft. Cliffhanger: a helicopter answers — and lands on the wrong
   hill, across the flooded creek.
6. **DAY 4: THE CREEK.** Reaching the helicopter means crossing debris-choked floodwater. Mags forbids it; Drizzle
   finds the long way around and learns "turn around, don't drown," six inches of moving water, and avoiding downed
   lines. Lessons: never walk or drive through floodwater; downed power lines; route planning. Cliffhanger: the long way
   is blocked by a landslide — with the school's missing aide on the far side.
7. **DAY 5: THE NEIGHBORS.** The hilltop camp starts to fray: cold, injuries, a frightened kid, an argument over food.
   Drizzle and Mags set up shifts, warmth, sanitation and a simple first-aid station; Mags admits she was scared on Day 0.
   Lessons: staying warm and dry (hypothermia), sanitation, checking on vulnerable neighbors. Cliffhanger: Mags collapses
   at the radio — her heart medication went down with her house.
8. **DAY 6: THE CRANK.** With Mags out of medicine, Drizzle has to get the message out alone: cranking the radio all
   night, relaying through a fishing boat, guiding a supply drop. Mags pulls through; the first boat of relief arrives.
   Lessons: keep a week's supply of medications, two-week readiness, how relief actually arrives. **Season cliffhanger:**
   as the relief boat docks, the radio crackles — USGS reports the aftershock sequence is migrating north, and the
   offshore buoy at the harbor mouth just registered a rise in sea level. Drizzle pulls his hood tighter. *"…Here we go."*
