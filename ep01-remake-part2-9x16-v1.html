When the shaking stopped, the kitchen was full of broken glass and one very loud question.
Now what?
Gull Harbor sits right on the coast.
And on the coast, a quake that long means one thing.
The ocean is coming.
MAGS"We're not waiting for a siren."
She was right. On the coast, the shaking is the warning.
There may never be a siren.
Drizzle grabbed his laptop. His photo album. A plant.
Then he put them all down.
The go-bag by the door had everything that mattered. Water.
A flashlight. A radio. Medicine. A warm layer.
Packed months ago, for exactly this.
It took three seconds to grab.
Outside, Gull Harbor looked wrong. Windows shattered.
Power lines down.
The street cracked open like a dropped plate.
And down at the harbor, something stranger.
The water was leaving.
Boats sat in the mud. Fish flopped on the sand.
A few people walked out to look.
That's the deadliest mistake on the coast.
When the sea pulls back, it comes back bigger.
Cars were already jammed on Harbor Way. Horns blaring.
Going nowhere.
Cracked roads. Fallen lines. Gridlock.
Feet don't get stuck in traffic.
Drizzle and Mags walked uphill.
