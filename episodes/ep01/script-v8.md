# RAINY DAY: CASCADIA — Episode 1: "DAY 0: THE SHAKING" — Script (v8, survival-guide revamp)

v8 retells v7 as a **survival guide told through Drizzle's story**: the narrator explains *why* he does each thing, with the
numbers behind it (the reference format: "1,200 a day = 30 days"). Same events and lessons as v7. Style direction: the user's
reference (clean colored cartoon plates, thin stick figure to scale, dark-gray icon cards, "DAY N" cards, radio bubbles,
one-word captions, brick-wall vertical frame with a title box and a "Part N" box).

Markers: N: narrator · CARD: full-frame dark-gray card with a small colored icon (icon in parentheses) · DAY: day/time card ·
BUBBLE: comic speech bubble (no spoken dialogue) · INSERT: prop close-up · ===PART=== TikTok part break.

Facts used (public guidance — WA EMD, NOAA, Ready.gov, Great ShakeOut): a full Cascadia rupture can shake for 3–5 minutes;
on parts of the outer coast the first wave can arrive in about 15 minutes; sirens and alerts can fail when power is out;
the sea drawing back is a natural tsunami warning; tsunamis are a series of waves for hours and the first is often not the
largest; drop, cover, hold on (not the doorway); 1 gallon of water per person per day; WA EMD asks households to be
"2 Weeks Ready". Flagged as story choices, not facts: the 0.4-mile / 12-minute walk and the 22-minute arrival time in
Gull Harbor (fictional town).

[COLD OPEN — 2 s: the wave cresting over Gull Harbor rooftops. Hard cut to black.]

N: This is Drizzle. He lives in Gull Harbor, Washington. Two thousand people, one road out, and one of the most dangerous fault lines in North America just off the coast.
DAY: DAY 0 · 10:42 AM (clock icon)
N: Drizzle wasn't a prepper. But one fact had stuck with him. On this coast, if the ground shakes hard, the ocean can arrive in about fifteen minutes.
CARD: FIRST WAVE: ~15 MINUTES (stopwatch + wave icon)
N: At 10:42 on a Tuesday, his coffee started to shiver. Then the floor moved.
N: His first instinct was the doorway. Most people's is. But in a modern house, a doorway is no stronger than the rest of the wall, and it's exactly where things fall.
N: So he did what the drills teach.
CARD: DROP · COVER · HOLD ON (table icon)
N: Drop before the quake drops you. Cover your head under something sturdy. Hold on to it, because the table will move, and you need to move with it.
N: Mags, his neighbor, was already under there. She'd come over for coffee. She'd done this drill every October for thirty years.
N: Ten seconds. Thirty. A minute. Small quakes end fast. This one kept going.
CARD: 4 MINUTES (stopwatch icon)
N: Four minutes of shaking. That told Drizzle everything. This was the big one. And the clock in his head started counting down.
N: Then the lights went out.
===PART=== (end Part 1)

N: The shaking stopped at 10:46. If the ocean came in fifteen minutes, it would arrive around 11:01. The walk to high ground took twelve.
CARD: 15 MIN − 12 MIN WALK = 3 MINUTES (stopwatch icon)
N: Three minutes. No time to pack a life. No time to look for keys or check the news.
N: He grabbed his laptop. His photo album. A plant. Then he put them all down.
CARD: GO-BAG, NOT BELONGINGS (backpack icon)
N: The go-bag by the door was packed for exactly this. Water, a flashlight, a radio, medicine, a warm layer. One bag. Three seconds.
BUBBLE (Mags): "No siren's coming. We go now."
N: She was right. With the power out, the sirens might never sound. On this coast, the shaking is the warning.
N: Outside, the street had cracked open. And down at the harbor, the water was leaving.
CARD: SEA PULLS BACK = TSUNAMI COMING (wave icon)
N: Boats sat in the mud. A few people walked out to look. That's the deadliest mistake on the coast. When the sea pulls back, it comes back bigger.
N: Harbor Way was already jammed. One crack in the road can stop a thousand cars. It can't stop two people on foot.
CARD: WALK, DON'T DRIVE (footprints icon)
N: So they walked. Uphill. Right now.
===PART=== (end Part 2)

N: The evacuation route was point four miles of steep, muddy trail. Twelve minutes, on a good day.
CARD: 0.4 MILES UPHILL = 12 MINUTES (arrow-up icon)
N: Halfway up, an aftershock hit. No table out here. So they got low, covered their heads, and stayed clear of the trees.
N: Then Mags' knees gave out. Drizzle didn't argue. He gave her his shoulder, and they kept moving. Slower, but moving.
INSERT: the hand-crank radio.
BUBBLE (radio): "...tsunami warning for the entire Washington coast... move to high ground immediately..."
N: The official warning came twenty minutes after the shaking started. Anyone who waited for it was already out of time.
N: At the switchback, Drizzle looked back. A gray line stretched across the horizon.
DAY: DAY 0 · 11:04 AM (clock icon)
N: At 11:04, the first wave hit Gull Harbor. It took the dock. The ferry. Then Main Street.
CARD: THE FIRST WAVE IS OFTEN NOT THE BIGGEST (three-waves icon)
N: And the first wave is rarely the last. Tsunamis come in sets, for hours. You don't go back down until officials say it's safe.
===PART=== (end Part 3)

N: The hilltop was cold, crowded, and above the water. Drizzle checked what he had.
CARD: 1 GALLON A DAY × 3 DAYS (water-jug icon)
N: Water first. One gallon per person, per day. His bag held three days.
CARD: 6,000 CALORIES = 3 DAYS (energy-bar + can icons)
N: Food second. About two thousand calories a day, for three days. That's the minimum. Out here, they tell you to plan for two weeks.
CARD: BE 2 WEEKS READY (calendar icon)
N: Drizzle counted what he'd done right.
CARD: 1 · DROP, COVER, HOLD ON (table icon)
CARD: 2 · COAST + SHAKING = WALK UPHILL NOW (arrow-up icon)
CARD: 3 · GO-BAG, NOT BELONGINGS (backpack icon)
N: One. Drop, cover, hold on. Two. On the coast, the shaking is your warning. Walk uphill, now. Three. Grab the go-bag, not your stuff.
N: And four. He'd packed snacks. Mags took one without a word.
N: Then the radio spoke again.
BUBBLE (radio): "...Highway 101 bridge at Gull Creek has collapsed... coastal roads closed..."
N: The only road out was gone. The whole coast had been hit at once, so help would come by air and sea, and it would take days.
BUBBLE (Drizzle): "So... nobody's coming?"
N: Below them, the water was still rising. Night was coming. And somewhere down in the dark, a light flickered on where no one should be.
