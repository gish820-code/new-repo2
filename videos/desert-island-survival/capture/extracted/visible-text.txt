Source facts (general survival guidance):
- The "rule of threes": a person can survive roughly 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in harsh conditions, 3 days without water, and 3 weeks without food. Priorities follow from it.
- Stay calm and take stock first (STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan). Salvage anything useful from the wreck or shoreline.
- Shelter: get out of the sun and wind; build above the high-tide line, use palm fronds and driftwood; shade lowers water loss.
- Water: never drink seawater — the salt dehydrates you faster. Collect rainwater (palm leaves, containers), drink from green coconuts, use a solar still (hole, container, plastic sheet, pebble) to condense fresh water.
- Fire: purifies water, cooks food, gives warmth, keeps insects away, and signals rescuers. Methods: friction (bow drill), lens/glass to focus sunlight. Gather tinder (dry coconut husk), kindling, fuel.
- Food: lowest priority. Shellfish, crabs, fish caught in tide pools, coconuts. Cook everything. Avoid unknown plants and berries.
- Signal: make yourself visible. Big SOS letters on the beach (rocks, logs, seaweed), keep three signal fires ready (three in a triangle is an international distress signal), mirror flashes toward ships and planes.
- Mindset: routine and hope keep you going until rescue.
