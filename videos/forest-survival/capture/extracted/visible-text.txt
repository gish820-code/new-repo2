Source facts (general wilderness survival guidance):
- When lost: STOP — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan. Don't keep wandering; most lost people are found close to where they went missing. Stay put.
- Shelter first: hypothermia is the main danger, even in mild weather at night. Debris hut: ridgepole propped on a stump or fork, sticks leaned along both sides as ribs, then a thick pile of leaves (about an arm's length thick) for insulation. Insulate from the ground too.
- Water: follow the terrain downhill to find streams. Boil water (rolling boil) to kill pathogens before drinking.
- Fire: gather tinder (dry grass, birch bark), kindling (pencil-thin sticks), and fuel (wrist-thick). Fire gives warmth, dries clothes, purifies water, and boosts morale.
- Signal: three of anything is a distress signal — three whistle blasts. Spread bright colors in a clearing. Smoke by day, fire by night.
- Food is a low priority for short emergencies.
